<header class="sg-hero">
<span class="sg-kicker">AMDE 350 · Social Design</span>
<h1>Design for<br/>Social Good.</h1>
<p class="sg-intro">
      Design can do more than make something look good. It can make people
      notice an issue, question an assumption, reconsider their behavior,
      and take action. In this project, you will use visual communication
      to bring attention to a social issue that matters.
    </p>
<div class="sg-meta">
<span>Research + Concept</span>
<span>Typography + Image</span>
<span>Poster Design</span>
<span>Critique + Revision</span>
</div>
</header>
<section class="sg-section">
<div class="sg-section-label">01 / The Challenge</div>
<h2>Make people stop and think.</h2>
<p>
      Your task is to design a poster that communicates a social issue
      clearly and persuasively. Choose an issue that deserves greater
      attention, research it, and develop a visual message that encourages
      an audience to think differently or take meaningful action.
    </p>
<p>
      Your design should combine a strong concept with intentional choices
      in typography, imagery, composition, color, and visual hierarchy.
      The goal is not simply to illustrate a problem. It is to communicate
      why the issue matters.
    </p>
<div class="sg-callout">
<p><strong>Your central question:</strong></p>
<p>
        How can a single poster make an audience notice, understand, or
        respond to a social issue?
      </p>
</div>
</section>

<section class="sg-section">
<div class="sg-section-label">03 / Research + Concept</div>
<h2>Choose an issue that matters.</h2>
<p>
      Begin by selecting a social, cultural, environmental, or public-interest
      issue. Your subject should be specific enough to communicate in a
      single poster and meaningful enough to support research and a clear
      point of view.
    </p>
<h3>Possible directions</h3>
<ul class="sg-list">
<li>Environmental responsibility, waste, or pollution</li>
<li>Public health, food systems, or access to resources</li>
<li>Equality, discrimination, or representation</li>
<li>Community safety, housing, or education</li>
<li>Digital privacy, misinformation, or online behavior</li>
<li>Animal welfare or protection of natural habitats</li>
<li>Another social issue approved by your instructor</li>
</ul>
<h3>Research before designing</h3>
<ul class="sg-list">
<li>Identify the problem and the people or communities affected.</li>
<li>Use credible sources to understand the issue.</li>
<li>Find a specific fact, insight, or misconception worth communicating.</li>
<li>Identify the audience you want to reach.</li>
<li>Decide what you want that audience to think, feel, or do.</li>
</ul>
<p>
      Keep a record of your sources. Avoid relying on a headline or statistic
      without checking its original context.
    </p>
</section>
<section class="sg-section">
<div class="sg-section-label">04 / Design Process</div>
<h2>Develop the idea before polishing it.</h2>
<div class="sg-steps">
<div class="sg-step">
<span class="sg-step-number">STEP 01</span>
<h3>Familiarize</h3>
<p>
          Research the issue, audience, context, and existing campaigns.
          Identify what your audience needs to understand.
        </p>
</div>
<div class="sg-step">
<span class="sg-step-number">STEP 02</span>
<h3>Explore</h3>
<p>
          Sketch multiple concepts. Experiment with headlines, metaphors,
          imagery, composition, and visual approaches.
        </p>
</div>
<div class="sg-step">
<span class="sg-step-number">STEP 03</span>
<h3>Refine</h3>
<p>
          Select the strongest concept. Develop hierarchy, readability,
          contrast, alignment, and the relationship between words and images.
        </p>
</div>
<div class="sg-step">
<span class="sg-step-number">STEP 04</span>
<h3>Evaluate</h3>
<p>
          Present your work for critique. Test whether viewers understand
          the message and revise based on useful feedback.
        </p>
</div>
</div>
<div class="sg-callout">
<p>
<strong>Important:</strong> Document your process. Your early sketches
        and discarded ideas help demonstrate how your final solution developed.
        Do not submit only the finished poster.
      </p>
</div>
</section>
<section class="sg-section">
<div class="sg-section-label">05 / Final Poster</div>
<h2>Design requirements.</h2>
<p>Your final poster must:</p>
<ul class="sg-list">
<li>Communicate one clear social message or central idea.</li>
<li>Use typography and imagery intentionally.</li>
<li>Establish a strong visual hierarchy and readable text.</li>
<li>Use composition, scale, color, and contrast to guide attention.</li>
<li>Include a call to action or a meaningful next step.</li>
<li>Credit any borrowed imagery, research, or other source material.</li>
<li>Be original work created for this assignment.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Format</h3>
<ul class="sg-list">
<li>
<strong>Poster size:</strong> 10 × 15 inches or 15 × 20 inches,
        following the original AIGA project brief.
      </li>
<li>
<strong>Final artwork:</strong> High-resolution PDF suitable for
        presentation or printing.
      </li>
<li>
<strong>Process document:</strong> A low-resolution PDF documenting
        research, sketches, concept development, and refinement.
      </li>
</ul>
<p>
      Your call to action may take the form of a website, organization name,
      hashtag, resource, or direct request. Make sure it fits the issue and
      gives the audience a meaningful way to respond.
    </p>
</section>
<section class="sg-section">
<div class="sg-section-label">06 / What to Submit</div>
<h2>Your deliverables.</h2>
<div class="sg-deliverable">
<h3><span class="sg-number">1</span>Research + Concept</h3>
<p>
        A concise explanation of the issue, intended audience, key insight,
        and message you want to communicate. Include links or citations for
        your research.
      </p>
</div>
<div class="sg-deliverable">
<h3><span class="sg-number">2</span>Process PDF</h3>
<p>
        Include research, initial sketches or thumbnails, alternate concepts,
        design development, and evidence of refinement. Export a low-resolution
        PDF that is easy to review.
      </p>
</div>
<div class="sg-deliverable">
<h3><span class="sg-number">3</span>Final Poster PDF</h3>
<p>
        Submit your finished poster at the required dimensions as a
        high-resolution PDF. Check image quality, typography, margins,
        and readability before exporting.
      </p>
</div>
<div class="sg-deliverable">
<h3><span class="sg-number">4</span>Critique + Reflection</h3>
<p>
        Be prepared to present your concept, explain your design decisions,
        respond to peer feedback, and identify revisions you made or would
        make next.
      </p>
</div>
</section>
<section class="sg-section">
<div class="sg-section-label">07 / Critique</div>
<h2>Evaluate the communication.</h2>
<p>
      During critique, we will consider not just whether the poster looks
      polished, but whether its design choices help communicate the issue.
    </p>
<ul class="sg-questions">
<li>What do you notice first, and why?</li>
<li>Can you identify the main message quickly?</li>
<li>Who is the intended audience?</li>
<li>How do typography and imagery reinforce the concept?</li>
<li>Does the poster communicate responsibly and accurately?</li>
<li>Is the call to action clear and appropriate?</li>
<li>What might confuse, distract, or alienate the audience?</li>
<li>What specific revision could make the design more effective?</li>
</ul>
</section>
<section class="sg-section">
<div class="sg-section-label">08 / Reflection</div>
<h2>Design has consequences.</h2>
<p>
      After critique, reflect on how your understanding of the issue shaped
      your design. Think about how graphic design can influence awareness,
      interpretation, and action—and where the responsibility of a designer
      begins.
    </p>
<h3>Respond to these prompts</h3>
<ul class="sg-list">
<li>What is the most important idea you want viewers to take away?</li>
<li>Which design decision best supports your message?</li>
<li>How did research change your initial concept?</li>
<li>What did you learn from critique?</li>
<li>What would you revise if you had more time?</li>
<li>How can designers advocate for change without oversimplifying an issue?</li>
</ul>
</section>
<section class="sg-section">
<div class="sg-section-label">09 / Assessment</div>
<h2>What makes the project successful?</h2>
<ul class="sg-list">
<li>
<strong>Concept and research:</strong> The issue is well understood,
        the message is focused, and the design is informed by credible research.
      </li>
<li>
<strong>Visual communication:</strong> Typography, imagery, hierarchy,
        composition, and contrast work together effectively.
      </li>
<li>
<strong>Audience and action:</strong> The poster considers its audience
        and provides an appropriate call to action or next step.
      </li>
<li>
<strong>Process and refinement:</strong> The work shows exploration,
        thoughtful iteration, and response to critique.
      </li>
<li>
<strong>Craft and delivery:</strong> The final PDF is clear, polished,
        correctly formatted, and accompanied by the required process documentation.
      </li>
</ul>
</section>
