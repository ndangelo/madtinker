<h1>Final Website Presentation<br/>&amp; Portfolio Case Study</h1>
<p>Present the finished experience. Explain the decisions. Reflect on what you learned.</p>
<div class="ag-chips">
<span class="ag-chip">Final Project</span>
<span class="ag-chip">Presentation</span>
<span class="ag-chip">Peer Critique</span>
<span class="ag-chip">Portfolio Case Study</span>
</div>
</header>
<section class="ag-section">
<h2>01 / Learning Objectives</h2>
<p>By the end of this lesson, you should be able to present a coherent website for a defined audience and goal, explain design decisions using process and testing evidence, and reflect on the development process.</p>
<div class="ag-grid">
<div class="ag-card"><h3>Present the Work</h3><p>Tell a clear story about the audience, purpose, and finished website.</p></div>
<div class="ag-card"><h3>Explain Decisions</h3><p>Connect visual design, structure, responsive behavior, and interaction to the project goals.</p></div>
<div class="ag-card"><h3>Reflect &amp; Revise</h3><p>Identify what changed, what you learned, and what you would improve next.</p></div>
</div>
</section>
<section class="ag-section">
<h2>02 / A Website Is More Than Its Final Screen</h2>
<p>A strong final project connects audience, purpose, content, visual direction, interaction, and responsive behavior. Your presentation should make the design process visible—not simply show a collection of screenshots.</p>
<div class="ag-grid">
<div class="ag-card"><h3>The Challenge</h3><p>Who is the site for? What need, question, or goal does it address?</p></div>
<div class="ag-card"><h3>The Approach</h3><p>How did research, content planning, wireframes, and visual choices shape the solution?</p></div>
<div class="ag-card"><h3>The Outcome</h3><p>What does the finished site do, and how does it behave across screen sizes?</p></div>
<div class="ag-card"><h3>The Reflection</h3><p>What changed after feedback or testing? What would you improve with more time?</p></div>
</div>
<blockquote><strong>Design principle:</strong> Show evidence for your decisions. Be clear about what you actually tested, what remains unfinished, and what you would improve next.</blockquote>
</section>
<section class="ag-section">
<h2>03 / Build a 5–7 Slide Presentation</h2>
<div class="ag-grid">
<div class="ag-card"><h3>01 — Audience &amp; Goal</h3><p>Introduce the project, intended audience, and the problem or opportunity.</p></div>
<div class="ag-card"><h3>02 — Process &amp; Direction</h3><p>Show a wireframe, research insight, or early design direction that influenced the outcome.</p></div>
<div class="ag-card"><h3>03 — Visual System</h3><p>Explain typography, color, composition, image choices, and reusable components.</p></div>
<div class="ag-card"><h3>04 — Final Website</h3><p>Show key pages and explain the main user journey or interaction.</p></div>
<div class="ag-card"><h3>05 — Responsive Checks</h3><p>Compare desktop and mobile views; discuss accessibility, navigation, and testing.</p></div>
<div class="ag-card"><h3>06–07 — Reflection</h3><p>Share a revision made after feedback, a challenge, and the next improvement you would make.</p></div>
</div>
</section>
<section class="ag-section">
<h2>04 / Instructor Demonstration</h2>
<p>Walk through the project like a designer. Open the site in a browser and demonstrate its primary user journey. Resize the browser to show how the layout adapts at different viewport widths. Open the project in your code editor and identify one meaningful HTML/CSS relationship. Describe one revision made after critique, testing, or troubleshooting.</p>
<div class="ag-card">
<h3>Optional CodePen Demonstration</h3>
<p>Add the public CodePen embed for this week once the Pen exists. In WordPress, paste CodePen’s official embed snippet into a Custom HTML block. Do not publish a placeholder embed.</p>
</div>
</section>
<section class="ag-section">
<h2>05 / Quality Assurance Checklist</h2>
<div class="ag-grid">
<div class="ag-card">
<h3>Function &amp; Structure</h3>
<ul>
<li>Navigation and links work across pages.</li>
<li>HTML pages have meaningful, consistent structure.</li>
<li>CSS and images load using correct paths.</li>
<li>No obvious broken layouts or missing assets.</li>
</ul>
</div>
<div class="ag-card">
<h3>Responsive &amp; Responsible</h3>
<ul>
<li>Desktop and mobile widths have been checked.</li>
<li>Text contrast and keyboard focus have been reviewed.</li>
<li>Images and third-party assets are credited.</li>
<li>Known limitations are documented honestly.</li>
</ul>
</div>
</div>
</section>
<section class="ag-section">
<h2>06 / Peer Critique</h2>
<p>Give feedback that leads to a useful next step. Be specific about what you noticed, how it affects the experience, and what the designer could consider next.</p>
<div class="ag-grid">
<div class="ag-card"><h3>Strength</h3><p>What is the website’s clearest strength, and what makes it effective?</p></div>
<div class="ag-card"><h3>Audience</h3><p>Where might the intended audience need more guidance?</p></div>
<div class="ag-card"><h3>Responsive Behavior</h3><p>What changes when you view the site at a mobile width?</p></div>
<div class="ag-card"><h3>Next Improvement</h3><p>What single improvement would have the greatest impact?</p></div>
</div>
</section>
<section class="ag-section">
<h2>07 / Final Deliverables</h2>
<div class="ag-card">
<ul>
<li><strong>Website source:</strong> a clear project folder with HTML pages, CSS, and assets.</li>
<li><strong>At least three meaningful pages total:</strong> consistently navigable and connected to the project goal.</li>
<li><strong>README:</strong> project purpose, audience, page list, folder map, live URL if available, and known limitations.</li>
<li><strong>Credits:</strong> attribution for images, fonts, libraries, and other third-party assets as needed.</li>
<li><strong>Testing evidence:</strong> desktop/mobile screenshots and a short QA checklist.</li>
<li><strong>Final presentation:</strong> 5–7 slides covering the challenge, process, design, outcome, and reflection.</li>
<li><strong>Final reflection:</strong> 250–400 words describing a design decision, challenge, feedback-driven change, and future improvement.</li>
</ul>
<p><em>Follow the instructor’s submission directions if they specify a different file structure or delivery method.</em></p>
</div>
</section>
<section class="ag-section">
<h2>08 / In-Class Exercise</h2>
<ol>
<li>Present your website and explain its audience, goal, and strongest design decision.</li>
<li>Demonstrate one responsive behavior or important interaction.</li>
<li>Identify one challenge and one change made after feedback or testing.</li>
<li>Collect peer feedback and select one useful next step.</li>
<li>Write your final reflection while the process is still fresh.</li>
</ol>
</section>
<section class="ag-section">
<h2>09 / Exit Ticket</h2>
<div class="ag-card">
<p>Write down one change you made, one thing you learned from the result, and one question or improvement you would carry into your next web project.</p>
</div>
</section>

<div class="ag-eyebrow">Final Takeaway</div>
<h2>Make the process visible.</h2>
<p>A strong presentation connects audience, decisions, evidence, and outcome. A strong portfolio case study shows not only what you made, but how and why the work developed.</p>
