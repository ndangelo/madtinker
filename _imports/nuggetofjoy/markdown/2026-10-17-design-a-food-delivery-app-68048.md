<p><strong>Client:</strong> Choose your own fictional restaurant, food-delivery service, or app brand. Use AI only when it directly supports the assignment requirements. Do not add unrelated features or extra screens.</p>

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">1. Project objectives</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Design a food-delivery app that allows users to browse restaurants, choose meals, customize their orders, pay securely, and have food delivered to their doorstep. The experience should be user-friendly, visually appealing, accessible, and efficient.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:html -->
<div class="food-app-assignment"><div class="quick-facts"><div class="fact"><strong>Find</strong><span>Discover restaurants and food that fit the user’s needs.</span></div><div class="fact"><strong>Order</strong><span>Customize meals and complete checkout without confusion.</span></div><div class="fact"><strong>Follow</strong><span>Understand order status and estimated delivery time.</span></div></div><div class="note"><strong>Design reminder:</strong> Do not design screens just to make the app look impressive. Every screen should help users complete a task.</div></div>
<!-- /wp:html -->
<!-- wp:heading -->

<!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Use the sample personas below to guide your design decisions. They represent different user priorities; your app should make it easy for each person to find appropriate options.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">3. App requirements</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:list {"className":"wp-block-list"} -->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Let users browse restaurants available in their area.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->
<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Allow users to customize orders, add special instructions, and identify dietary needs.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->
<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Show order status and estimated delivery time.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->
<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Provide a clear, secure, easy-to-use payment experience.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->
<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Allow users to review and rate restaurants and delivery drivers.</li>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">6. Required deliverable: User flow in FigJam</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Before designing your screens, map the user journey in FigJam.</strong> Your user flow should show the main decisions and actions a person takes to find food and place an order. Use arrows to connect steps, and include a decision point when the user might change direction.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:html -->
<div class="food-app-assignment"><div aria-label="Example food delivery user flow from opening the app through delivery tracking" class="flow-visual" role="img"><div class="flow-step"><strong>01 · Start</strong>Open app / sign in</div><div class="flow-arrow">→</div><div class="flow-step"><strong>02 · Discover</strong>Search or browse restaurants</div><div class="flow-arrow">→</div><div class="flow-step"><strong>03 · Choose</strong>Select restaurant and meal</div><div class="flow-arrow">→</div><div class="flow-step"><strong>04 · Customize</strong>Options, quantity, instructions</div><div class="flow-arrow">→</div><div class="flow-step"><strong>05 · Review</strong>Check cart, fees, and total</div><div class="flow-arrow">→</div><div class="flow-step"><strong>06 · Checkout</strong>Address and payment</div><div class="flow-arrow">→</div><div class="flow-step"><strong>07 · Complete</strong>Confirmation and tracking</div></div><div class="note"><strong>Include at least one decision:</strong> What happens if a meal is unavailable, the user changes the cart, or payment fails? Show how the user can recover instead of getting stuck.</div></div>
<!-- /wp:html -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Submit:</strong> a FigJam user-flow diagram that is readable, logically connected, and consistent with the seven screens you will design. Start from the provided course user-flow template if one has been supplied in D2L.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">02 — Build Your UI/UX Style Guide</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A style guide helps you make consistent design decisions before you build the final screens. It prevents you from choosing colors, type sizes, and button styles independently on every screen.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><a href="https://www.figma.com/design/pMDnezhhqour9AwJLK3G0Y">Open the Figma UI/UX Style Guide Template →</a></p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Use the actual course template shown below.</strong> These screenshots are from the Student UI/UX Style Guide template used in class. Duplicate the Figma file, then complete these same sections for your Uber holiday-ride redesign. The examples farther down this page are supplementary demonstrations, not replacement templates.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->

<!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Collect three visual references that help establish your design direction. These might include mobile interfaces, typography, color palettes, maps, transportation apps, or other relevant visual material. Add a short note explaining what you want to learn from each reference.</p>

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Foundations</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Define the product's purpose, intended user, primary task, and visual direction. Your decisions should support the user flow you created.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img alt="Actual Figma Student UI/UX Style Guide section: foundations" src="https://www.figma.com/api/mcp/asset/c53b8af3-9dd9-49b8-b29c-af6053d039aa.png"/><figcaption class="wp-element-caption"><strong>Actual course template — Foundations.</strong> Use this section to establish the project before designing screens.</figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->
<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Color and contrast</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Establish a small, purposeful color system. Identify the role of each color, including primary actions, backgrounds, text, and error states. Check that text and interface elements remain readable. Color should communicate meaning clearly rather than serve only as decoration.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img alt="Actual Figma Student UI/UX Style Guide section: color" src="https://www.figma.com/api/mcp/asset/dc6c23d3-e208-4de1-80b0-81999bb35d97.png"/><figcaption class="wp-element-caption"><strong>Actual course template — Color.</strong> Apply the color roles to your own redesign and check contrast.</figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->
<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Typography and spacing</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Create a consistent type hierarchy for headings, body text, and captions. Establish spacing values for padding inside elements and gaps between elements.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img alt="Actual Figma Student UI/UX Style Guide section: type" src="https://www.figma.com/api/mcp/asset/109fd972-a72f-404c-900c-9e0458a899bc.png"/><figcaption class="wp-element-caption"><strong>Actual course template — Typography &amp; Spacing.</strong> Use these type and spacing rules consistently across the interface.</figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>For example, you might use 16 px inside a card and 24 px between cards. The important thing is to apply your choices consistently.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Components</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Define reusable interface elements, such as buttons, input fields, cards, and navigation. Components should look and behave consistently throughout the app.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img alt="Actual Figma Student UI/UX Style Guide section: components" src="https://www.figma.com/api/mcp/asset/b2b52fc0-fe1f-42b8-9df3-54546214301f.png"/><figcaption class="wp-element-caption"><strong>Actual course template — Components.</strong> Adapt these reusable elements to your product and document their behavior.</figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->
<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Interaction states and accessibility</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Consider what happens when a user interacts with an element. Depending on the component, relevant states may include default, hover, focus, pressed, disabled, error, or success.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img alt="Actual Figma Student UI/UX Style Guide section: states" src="https://www.figma.com/api/mcp/asset/e95b4662-5403-4563-a3dd-3ad472b300f7.png"/><figcaption class="wp-element-caption"><strong>Actual course template — States.</strong> Document how interactive elements respond to different states.</figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Check contrast, text readability, visible focus indicators, clear labels, and whether meaning depends on color alone.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Deliverable:</strong> Complete the relevant sections of the Figma style guide and keep it available while designing your prototype.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
<!-- wp:image -->
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img alt="Actual Figma Student UI/UX Style Guide section: Accessibility" src="https://www.figma.com/api/mcp/asset/6efc91f7-9a9f-4de0-80c3-20521f419f83.png"/><figcaption class="wp-element-caption"><strong>Actual course template — Accessibility.</strong> Check contrast, readable type, visible focus, clear labels, and distinct feedback.</figcaption></figure>
<!-- /wp:image -->
<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p><strong>Submit:</strong> a Figma Design style-guide page showing your color palette, typography, buttons, form fields, icons, spacing rules, and reusable components. The seven app screens should use these same styles.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">10. Suggested workflow</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->
<!-- wp:list {"ordered":true,"className":"wp-block-list"} -->
<ol class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Define the user.</strong> Choose which persona your design will prioritize and identify their main needs.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->
<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Map the user flow in FigJam.</strong> Diagram the path from opening the app to placing and tracking an order, including decision and recovery paths.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->
<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Create the UI/UX style guide in Figma Design.</strong> Establish colors, typography, buttons, form fields, spacing, icons, and reusable components.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->
<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Design the seven screens.</strong> Start with clear layouts and meaningful hierarchy before adding decorative details.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->
<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Check the full journey.</strong> Make sure users can move from discovering food to successful checkout and order tracking.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item -->
<!-- wp:list-item -->
<li><strong>Test and refine.</strong> Ask someone to complete a simple task using your prototype. Note where they hesitate and improve those areas.</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ol>
