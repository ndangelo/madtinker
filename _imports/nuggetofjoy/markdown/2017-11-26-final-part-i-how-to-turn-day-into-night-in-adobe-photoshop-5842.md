It's not so easy to find a perfect photo for a photo manipulation we have in mind. Sometimes, for example, we want to create a night scene, but the available photos are either too dark or already heavily edited. What do to in such a case?

Well, you can always turn a day scene into a night scene with the power of Photoshop! In this tutorial I will show you how to play with the brightness of the scene, how to add stars to the sky, and how to add new light sources with convincing effects.
<h2 class="nolinks">What You Will Need</h2>
You can use any photo you need for this exercise. If you want to follow my steps directly, you can find the same photo I use here:
<ul>
<li>Village photo (Pick your own)</li>
</ul>
<h2 class="nolinks"><span class="sectionnum">1.</span> How to Select the Sky</h2>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 1</h3>
Open your photo in Photoshop. Take the <strong>Magic Wand Tool (W) </strong>and change its <strong>Tolerance</strong> to<strong> 100</strong>. Caution: such a high tolerance works best with a good contrast between the sky and the buildings.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="magic wand photoshop tolerance" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-1-1-min.png"/></figure>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 2</h3>
Use the Magic Wand on the sky.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="magic wand tool sky selection" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-1-2-min.jpg"/></figure>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 3</h3>
Go into <strong>Quick Mask Mode (Q)</strong> to see the selection better. Pick any brush to paint the areas that aren't part of the sky (paint with black to select them as red).
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="quick mask mode selection" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-1-3-min.jpg"/>
<figcaption>The mountains in the background are affected by the color of the sky, so let's make them a part if it.</figcaption></figure>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 4</h3>
Exit <strong>Quick Mask Mode (Q)</strong> and go to <strong>Select &gt; Refine Edge</strong> to make sure the selection is perfect. Select a view that lets you see the effects the best.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="refine edge selection photoshop" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-1-4-min.png"/></figure>
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="refine edge on white view" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-1-5-min.jpg"/></figure>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 5</h3>
Now check <strong>Smart Radius</strong> and increase its value. You can also play with other sliders to get a perfect result. When you're done, click <strong>OK</strong>.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="refine edge smart radius" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-1-6-min.png"/></figure>
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="how to select with refine edge" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-1-7-min.jpg"/></figure>
<h2 class="nolinks"><span class="sectionnum">2.</span> How to Darken the Sky</h2>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 1</h3>
Go to <strong>Window &gt; Adjustments</strong>. Select <strong>Hue/Saturation</strong> from the panel.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="hue saturation adjustment layer" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-2-1-min.png"/></figure>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 2</h3>
<strong>Clip</strong> the Adjustment Layer to the layer below (the sky). Then change the<strong>Lightness</strong> to make the sky very dark, but not black.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="darken the sky saturation" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-2-2-min.png"/></figure>
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="dark sky adjustment photoshop" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-2-3-min.jpg"/></figure>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 3</h3>
Every Adjustment Layer has a Layer Mask. You can learn how it works from this quick tutorial:
<ul class="roundup-block__contents posts--half-width roundup-block--list">
<li class="roundup-block__content"><a class="roundup-block__content-link" href="http://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/quick-tip-layer-mask-vs-the-eraser-tool-in-adobe-photoshop--cms-24392"><img class="roundup-block__preview-image alignleft" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/eraser-vs-layer-mask-prev.jpg"/></a>
<div class="roundup-block__content-title">Quick Tip: Layer Mask vs. the Eraser Tool in Adobe Photoshop</div>
<div class="roundup-block__author">Monika Zagrobelna</div></li>
</ul>
In short, when you paint on a mask, you define the transparency of the layer: black makes the layer transparent, white makes it opaque, and the shades of gray become the states in between.

Click the mask to make it active.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="how layer mask work" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-2-4-min.png"/></figure>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 4</h3>
Take the <strong>Gradient Tool (G) </strong>and click the gradient in the upper panel.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="gradient tool change gradient" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-2-5-min.png"/></figure>
Change the gradient to very bright gray-white.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="bright gradient" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-2-6-min.png"/></figure>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 5</h3>
Apply the gradient to the mask from top to bottom. This will make the upper part "white" (opaque), and the lower part "bright gray" (slightly transparent).
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="bright gradient on layer mask" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-2-7-min.jpg"/></figure>
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="how to use gradient on layer mask" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-2-8-min.jpg"/></figure>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 6</h3>
Click the background layer now. We need to darken it too! In the <strong>Adjustments </strong>panel, find <strong>Photo Filter</strong>.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="photo filter adjustment layer" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-3-1-min.png"/></figure>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 7</h3>
Change the color to dark, desaturated blue.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="how to change color of photo filter" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-3-2-min.png"/></figure>
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="dark blue photo filter" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-3-3-min.png"/></figure>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 8</h3>
Uncheck <strong>Preserve Luminosity</strong>...
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="preserve luminosity photo filter" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-3-4-min.png"/></figure>
... and make the effect very intense.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="density photo filter" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-3-5-min.png"/></figure>
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="photo filter night effect" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-3-6-min.jpg"/></figure>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 9</h3>
The sky could use some blue tint as well! Add the <strong>Photo Filter </strong>adjustment to it, too, this time using a brighter, more saturated shade.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="bright blue photo filter" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-4-1-min.jpg"/></figure>
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="sublte photo filter" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-4-2-min.png"/>
<figcaption>Don't forget to clip the adjustment to affect the sky only!</figcaption></figure>
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="night photo filter" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-4-3-min.jpg"/></figure>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 10</h3>
Normally, the night sky is brighter than non-illuminated buildings below, so let's darken that lower layer some more.<strong> Duplicate (Control-J) </strong>the Photo Filter adjustment and lower its <strong>Opacity</strong> to adjust the intensity.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="duplicate adjustment layer" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-4-4-min.png"/></figure>
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="intense darkening photo filter" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-4-5-min.jpg"/></figure>
<h2 class="nolinks"><span class="sectionnum">3.</span> How to Create the Stars</h2>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 1</h3>
Create a <strong>New Layer</strong> on top. Use the <strong>Paint Bucket Tool (G)</strong> to fill it with black. Then go to <strong>Filter &gt; Noise &gt; Add Noise</strong>. Add a maximum amount of black-and-white noise.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="filter add noise" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-4-6-min.png"/></figure>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 2</h3>
Now go to <strong>Filter &gt; Filter Gallery</strong> and select <strong>Sketch &gt; Stamp</strong>. Play with the settings to create an optimal effect. The sky is not perfectly clear, so we don't want too many stars.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="how to make stars in photoshop" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-4-7-min.png"/></figure>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 3</h3>
Use the <strong>Move Tool (V)</strong> to move the stars up, over the sky.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="move sky up" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-4-8-min.jpg"/></figure>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 4</h3>
<strong>Right click</strong> the stars layer and select <strong>Blending Options</strong>. Drag the upper black marker to the middle, to make the dark part of the layer transparent.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="how to use blend if" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-4-9-min.png"/></figure>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 5</h3>
Add a <strong>Layer Mask</strong> to this layer. Use a black-and-white gradient to make the lower part transparent, gradually turning opaque towards the top.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="how to add layer mask" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-4-7-2-min.png"/></figure>
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="gradual change in opacity" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-4-10-min.jpg"/></figure>
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="layer effect added" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-4-11-min.jpg"/></figure>
<h2 class="nolinks"><span class="sectionnum">4.</span> How to Illuminate the Windows</h2>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 1</h3>
Let's pick some windows in the background—the farther they are, the harder it will be to notice the little imperfections. Use the <strong>Polygonal Lasso Tool (L)</strong> to select the panes. Hold <strong>Shift </strong>to keep selecting after closing one selection.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="how to select with polygonal lasso" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-5-1-min.jpg"/></figure>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 2</h3>
Click the background layer and <strong>duplicate</strong> the selection<strong> (Control-J)</strong>. Move the duplicated part to the top.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="drag layers" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-5-2-min.png"/></figure>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 3</h3>
<strong>Right click</strong> the layer and select <strong>Blending Options</strong>. Check <strong>Color Overlay</strong> and change the color to bright orange.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="blending options color overlay" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-5-3-min.png"/></figure>
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="bright orange color overlay" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-5-4-min.jpg"/></figure>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 4</h3>
Click the<strong> Blend Mode</strong> and scroll through it until you find the effect you like. I've decided to use <strong>Hue</strong>, as it adds the color without concealing the details.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="hue blend mode" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-5-5-min.png"/></figure>
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="bright windows" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-5-6-min.jpg"/></figure>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 5</h3>
Check <strong>Outer Glow</strong>.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="blending options outer glow" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-5-7-min.png"/></figure>
Change its color to bright orange.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="outer glow bright orange" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-5-8-min.jpg"/></figure>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 6</h3>
Play with the <strong>Blend Mode</strong> again...
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="how to use correct blend mode" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-5-9-min.png"/></figure>
... and adjust the sliders to the effect you want.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="how to adjust blending options" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-5-10-min.png"/></figure>
The effect should be quite subtle.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="bright windows in dark" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-5-11-min.jpg"/></figure>
<h2 class="nolinks"><span class="sectionnum">5.</span> How to Add a New Light Source</h2>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 1</h3>
There's something resembling a lantern on the side of one of the buildings. Let's use it to add some interesting light to the scene! Use the <strong>Polygonal Lasso Tool (L)</strong> to select its shape.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="select with lasso tool" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-6-1-min.jpg"/></figure>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 2</h3>
Click the background layer,<strong> duplicate</strong> the selection <strong>(Control-J)</strong>, and drag the new layer to the top.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="drag selection up" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-6-2-min1.jpg"/></figure>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 3</h3>
<strong>Right click</strong> the layer and select <strong>Blending Options</strong>. Check <strong>Color Overlay</strong> and make it white.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="white color overlay" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-6-3-min.png"/></figure>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 4</h3>
Check <strong>Outer Glow</strong> and make it bright orange.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="bright orange outer glow" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-6-4-min.png"/></figure>
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="select color for glow" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-6-5-min.jpg"/></figure>
Experiment with the settings to get the best result.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="outer glow blend mode" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-6-6-min.png"/></figure>

Create a <strong>New Layer</strong> and drag it right below the lantern. Pick bright orange and use a soft brush to paint the light around the lantern. You can lower the <strong>Flow </strong>to make the brush even softer.

<h2 class="nolinks"><span class="sectionnum">6.</span> How to Add a Late Dusk/Early Dawn Effect</h2>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 1</h3>
Let's adjust the final brightness of the scene now, since we have it all together. Go to the top of the layers and add a new Adjustment Layer: <strong>Levels</strong>.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="levels adjustment layer" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-7-1-min.png"/></figure>
Drag the white marker to the left to increase the amount of bright shades in the scene.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="more brightness in the scene" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-7-2-min.png"/></figure>
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="photo with leveles adjusted" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-7-3-min.jpg"/></figure>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 2</h3>
Create a <strong>New Layer</strong>. <strong>Fill</strong> it with black, and then go to its <strong>Blending Options</strong> and add <strong>Gradient Overlay</strong>.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="gradient overlay" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-7-4-min.png"/></figure>
Give it the colors of a low sun.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="sunset gradient" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-7-5-min.png"/></figure>
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="sunset sunrise color gradient" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-7-6-min.png"/></figure>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 3</h3>
Hold <strong>Control</strong> and click the sky layer to get its outline.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="how to take selection from layer" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-7-7-min.png"/></figure>
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="part of layer selected" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-7-8-min.jpg"/></figure>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 4</h3>
Click the gradient layer and add a <strong>Layer Mask</strong>. The selection will be automatically applied to the mask.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="selection applied to layer mask" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-7-9-min.jpg"/></figure>
Paint on the mask to reveal the mountains in the background.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="layer mask painting" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-7-10-min.jpg"/></figure>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 5</h3>
<strong>Duplicate</strong> the sky <strong>(Control-J)</strong> and drag it to the top. We're going to add special effects to the clouds.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="copy sky" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-7-11-min.jpg"/></figure>
Add a <strong>Hue/Saturation</strong> adjustment.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="hue saturation adjustments panel" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-7-12-min.png"/></figure>
Clip the <strong>Adjustment Layer</strong> to the duplicated sky, desaturate it, and make it slightly brighter.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="how to use hue saturation adjustment" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-7-13-min.png"/></figure>
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="desaturated sky" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-7-14-min.jpg"/></figure>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 6</h3>
Add a <strong>Levels</strong> adjustment, clip it, and add more contrast to the sky with it.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="levels contrast" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-7-16-min.png"/></figure>
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="contrasting clouds" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-7-15-min.jpg"/></figure>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 7</h3>
Select all three layers (the sky and its Adjustment Layers) and <strong>Merge </strong>them<strong>(Control-E)</strong>. Then go to the <strong>Blending Options</strong> of this new layer and remove it from the bright parts of the sky using the lower white marker. Change its <strong>Blend Mode</strong> to <strong>Soft Light</strong> and lower the <strong>Opacity</strong>.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="blend if adjusting transparency" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-7-18-min.png"/></figure>
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="sky adjusted" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-7-19-min.jpg"/></figure>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 8</h3>
<strong>Duplicate (Control-J)</strong> the layer and play with its <strong>Blending Options</strong> too, this time removing it from the dark parts of the sky (the lower black marker). Change the <strong>Blend Mode</strong> to <strong>Soft Light</strong> as well, but leave the <strong>Opacity</strong> at <strong>100%</strong>, adjusting it with a layer mask if needed.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="adjusting transparency of layer" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-7-20-min.png"/></figure>
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="layer transparency adjusted" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-7-22-min.jpg"/></figure>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 9</h3>
Time for some final adjustments. Add an <strong>Exposure </strong>adjustment on top to play with the overall brightness.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="exposure adjustment" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-7-23-min.png"/></figure>
I've also decided to drag the sky slightly down to make the change of the colors more gradual (you can do it with a smart use of Layer Masks).
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="final brightness adjustment" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-7-24-min.jpg"/></figure>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 10</h3>
The background seemed a little too bright, so I darkened it too using a <strong>Hue/Saturation</strong> adjustment with some masking.
<figure class="post_image"><img alt="darkening background" src="http://nuggetofjoy.com/wp-content/uploads/day-to-night-7-25-min.jpg"/></figure>
<h3 class="nolinks">Step 11</h3>
When you're done, <strong>merge</strong> all the layers (<strong>right click &gt; Flatten Image</strong>) and go to <strong>Noise &gt; Add Noise</strong>. This will add some realism of a photograph taken in low light.
