<h1>Final Critique, Revisions &amp; Website Submission</h1>
<p>You have spent the semester learning how to structure web content, create visual systems, build layouts, and adapt websites to different screens. This week, you will present your final website, exchange constructive feedback, test your work, and make thoughtful revisions before submitting the finished project.</p>
<div class="ag-chips">
<span class="ag-chip">Final critique</span>
<span class="ag-chip">Peer feedback</span>
<span class="ag-chip">Usability testing</span>
<span class="ag-chip">Final submission</span>
</div>
</header>
<section class="ag-section">
<h2>01 / The Purpose of the Final Critique</h2>
<p>A website is never just a collection of code. It is a designed experience that helps people find information, understand a message, and interact with content. The final critique gives you an opportunity to look at your work from another person's perspective.</p>
<p>You will review your own website, explore the websites created by classmates, and identify changes that could improve clarity, usability, visual consistency, and responsive behavior.</p>
<div class="ag-grid">
<div class="ag-card">
<div class="ag-number">01</div>
<h3>Present</h3>
<p>Explain your topic, intended audience, visual direction, and the purpose of your website.</p>
</div>
<div class="ag-card">
<div class="ag-number">02</div>
<h3>Evaluate</h3>
<p>Review how well the layout, navigation, content, and visual design work together.</p>
</div>
<div class="ag-card">
<div class="ag-number">03</div>
<h3>Revise</h3>
<p>Use feedback and testing to identify and correct problems in your website.</p>
</div>
<div class="ag-card">
<div class="ag-number">04</div>
<h3>Reflect</h3>
<p>Describe what you learned and how your design decisions changed throughout the semester.</p>
</div>
</div>
<div class="ag-callout">
<strong>Remember:</strong> A critique is not about whether someone personally likes your design. It is about understanding the design decisions, identifying what is working, and finding ways to make the website more effective for its intended audience.
    </div>
</section>
<section class="ag-section">
<h2>02 / Prepare Your Website for Review</h2>
<p>Before the critique begins, make sure your website is ready for someone else to explore. A classmate should be able to open your site, understand its purpose, and move between pages without needing you to explain how everything works.</p>
<div class="ag-checklist">
<ul>
<li>All required pages are present.</li>
<li>The navigation links connect the pages correctly.</li>
<li>The website has a clear visual identity.</li>
<li>Images and other media display correctly.</li>
<li>Text is complete, readable, and organized.</li>
<li>The layout adapts to mobile and desktop widths.</li>
<li>There are no obvious broken links or missing images.</li>
<li>The website is accessible through the link or files you intend to submit.</li>
</ul>
</div>
<div class="ag-callout">
<strong>Before sharing a link:</strong> Confirm that the link works for someone other than you. If your project is stored in a private workspace or requires a login, provide the instructor with an accessible version or the required project files.
    </div>
</section>
<section class="ag-section">
<h2>03 / Present Your Website</h2>
<p>Prepare a brief presentation of your final project. You do not need to explain every line of code. Focus on the purpose of the website, the audience, and the decisions you made as a designer.</p>
<h3>Your presentation should address</h3>
<ol class="ag-steps">
<li><strong>Concept:</strong> What is your website about, and why did you choose this topic?</li>
<li><strong>Audience:</strong> Who is the website designed for?</li>
<li><strong>Visual direction:</strong> How do your colors, typography, images, and layout support the subject?</li>
<li><strong>Structure:</strong> How did you organize the pages and navigation?</li>
<li><strong>Technical decisions:</strong> Where did you use CSS Grid, Flexbox, responsive media queries, or semantic HTML?</li>
<li><strong>Challenges:</strong> What was difficult, and how did you address it?</li>
<li><strong>Next steps:</strong> What would you improve if you had more time?</li>
</ol>
<div class="ag-callout">
<strong>Presentation tip:</strong> Show your website in action. Navigate between pages and demonstrate how the layout changes when you resize the browser window.
    </div>
</section>
<section class="ag-section">
<h2>04 / How to Give Constructive Feedback</h2>
<p>Useful critique feedback is specific, respectful, and connected to the purpose of the design. Avoid comments that only express personal preference, such as "I don't like that color." Explain what you noticed and how it affects the experience.</p>
<div class="ag-grid">
<div class="ag-card">
<h3>Observation</h3>
<p>Describe something you can see or experience on the website.</p>
<p><em>"The navigation links are easy to find at the top of each page."</em></p>
</div>
<div class="ag-card">
<h3>Question</h3>
<p>Ask about a design decision to better understand the creator's intention.</p>
<p><em>"How did you decide which projects should appear first in the gallery?"</em></p>
</div>
<div class="ag-card">
<h3>Suggestion</h3>
<p>Offer a specific change that might improve the experience.</p>
<p><em>"You might increase the space between the project cards so each one is easier to distinguish."</em></p>
</div>
</div>
<h3>Try the "I noticed / I wondered / I suggest" method</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>I noticed:</strong> Identify something that is working or something that may need attention.</li>
<li><strong>I wondered:</strong> Ask a question about the designer's intent.</li>
<li><strong>I suggest:</strong> Offer a possible next step or improvement.</li>
</ul>
<div class="ag-callout">
<strong>Keep it constructive.</strong> Critique the design, not the person. Feedback should help the designer make informed decisions, not require them to accept every suggestion.
    </div>
</section>
<section class="ag-section">
<h2>05 / Peer Critique Worksheet</h2>
<p>Use these questions when reviewing a classmate's website. Record specific observations rather than general statements.</p>
<div class="ag-card">
<h3>Purpose and audience</h3>
<ul>
<li>Can you quickly understand what the website is about?</li>
<li>Is the intended audience clear?</li>
<li>Does the content support the website's purpose?</li>
</ul>
</div>
<div class="ag-card">
<h3>Visual design</h3>
<ul>
<li>Are the colors and typography consistent?</li>
<li>Is the most important information easy to identify?</li>
<li>Do spacing and alignment make the content easy to scan?</li>
<li>Do the images support the subject and overall design?</li>
</ul>
</div>
<div class="ag-card">
<h3>Navigation and usability</h3>
<ul>
<li>Can you find and visit all the pages?</li>
<li>Do links communicate where they lead?</li>
<li>Is it easy to understand where you are within the website?</li>
</ul>
</div>
<div class="ag-card">
<h3>Responsive design and accessibility</h3>
<ul>
<li>Does the layout work at narrow and wide screen sizes?</li>
<li>Can you read the text comfortably?</li>
<li>Are images displayed correctly?</li>
<li>Are headings, links, and other content organized clearly?</li>
</ul>
</div>
<h3>Write your feedback</h3>
<div class="ag-checklist">
<p><strong>One strength:</strong> Identify one effective design decision and explain why it works.</p>
<p><strong>One question:</strong> Ask about something you would like to understand better.</p>
<p><strong>One suggestion:</strong> Recommend a specific change that could improve the website.</p>
</div>
</section>
<section class="ag-section">
<h2>06 / Revise Your Website</h2>
<p>After reviewing feedback, decide which changes will make the biggest difference. You do not need to implement every suggestion. Consider your website's purpose, the needs of its audience, and the time available.</p>
<div class="ag-grid">
<div class="ag-card">
<div class="ag-number">01</div>
<h3>Fix functional problems</h3>
<p>Correct broken links, missing images, incorrect file paths, and navigation problems first.</p>
</div>
<div class="ag-card">
<div class="ag-number">02</div>
<h3>Improve readability</h3>
<p>Adjust typography, spacing, contrast, and content organization where needed.</p>
</div>
<div class="ag-card">
<div class="ag-number">03</div>
<h3>Refine the layout</h3>
<p>Improve alignment, responsive behavior, and consistency between pages.</p>
</div>
<div class="ag-card">
<div class="ag-number">04</div>
<h3>Polish the details</h3>
<p>Review image crops, captions, headings, link styles, and other finishing touches.</p>
</div>
</div>
<h3>Prioritize your revisions</h3>
<p>Use this order when deciding what to fix:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Critical:</strong> Broken pages, missing content, or navigation that does not work.</li>
<li><strong>Important:</strong> Readability, responsive layout, accessibility, and unclear page structure.</li>
<li><strong>Polish:</strong> Small visual refinements and decorative details.</li>
</ol>
</section>
<section class="ag-section">
<h2>07 / Final Technical Check</h2>
<p>Before submitting your final website, test it as if you were a first-time visitor. Check every page and do not assume that something works simply because it looked correct while you were building it.</p>
<div class="ag-code-label">Responsive design reminders</div>
<pre class="ag-code"><code><span class="ag-text">/* Images should fit their containers */</span>
<span class="ag-tag">img</span> {
  <span class="ag-css-prop">max-width</span>: <span class="ag-css-value">100%</span>;
  <span class="ag-css-prop">height</span>: <span class="ag-css-value">auto</span>;
}

<span class="ag-text">/* Example of a mobile-first gallery */</span>
<span class="ag-tag">.gallery</span> {
  <span class="ag-css-prop">display</span>: <span class="ag-css-value">grid</span>;
  <span class="ag-css-prop">grid-template-columns</span>: <span class="ag-css-value">1fr</span>;
  <span class="ag-css-prop">gap</span>: <span class="ag-css-value">20px</span>;
}

<span class="ag-tag">@media (min-width: 600px)</span> {
  <span class="ag-tag">.gallery</span> {
    <span class="ag-css-prop">grid-template-columns</span>: <span class="ag-css-value">repeat(2, 1fr)</span>;
  }
}

<span class="ag-tag">@media (min-width: 900px)</span> {
  <span class="ag-tag">.gallery</span> {
    <span class="ag-css-prop">grid-template-columns</span>: <span class="ag-css-value">repeat(3, 1fr)</span>;
  }
}</code></pre>
<p>This is a reminder of the responsive techniques from Week 12, not a requirement to replace your own layout. Keep the CSS that best serves your design.</p>
<div class="ag-checklist">
<ul>
<li>Open every page and test every navigation link.</li>
<li>Check for missing images and incorrect file paths.</li>
<li>Preview the website at mobile, tablet, and desktop widths.</li>
<li>Look for horizontal scrolling and overlapping content.</li>
<li>Test keyboard navigation and visible focus indicators.</li>
<li>Check heading hierarchy, link descriptions, and image alt text.</li>
<li>Remove unfinished placeholder text and unused content.</li>
<li>Confirm that the final version is the version you submit.</li>
</ul>
</div>
</section>
<section class="ag-section">
<h2>08 / Final Reflection</h2>
<p>Reflect on your progress throughout the semester. Your reflection should discuss both the finished website and what you learned about web design.</p>
<h3>Reflection questions</h3>
<ul>
<li>What was the original idea behind your website?</li>
<li>Which design decision are you most satisfied with, and why?</li>
<li>What was the most challenging part of building the website?</li>
<li>How did peer feedback or testing influence your revisions?</li>
<li>What did you learn about HTML, CSS, responsive design, or accessibility?</li>
<li>If you continued working on this site, what would you improve next?</li>
</ul>
<div class="ag-callout">
<strong>Be specific.</strong> Instead of saying "I learned CSS," explain what you used CSS to accomplish, such as creating a responsive gallery, establishing a consistent visual identity, or improving the spacing and readability of your pages.
    </div>
</section>
<section class="ag-section">
<h2>09 / Final Submission Checklist</h2>
<p>Use this checklist to confirm that your project is ready to be evaluated.</p>
<div class="ag-checklist">
<ul>
<li>At least three connected pages are complete.</li>
<li>Navigation works throughout the website.</li>
<li>The website has a consistent visual identity.</li>
<li>The HTML structure and CSS styling are organized.</li>
<li>Images, text, and other content display correctly.</li>
<li>Responsive layouts work at mobile and desktop sizes.</li>
<li>Basic accessibility and usability checks are complete.</li>
<li>Peer feedback has been reviewed and appropriate revisions made.</li>
<li>The final reflection is complete.</li>
<li>The submitted URL or project files are accessible to the instructor.</li>
</ul>
</div>
</section>
