<h2>Week 01<br/>Introduction to Social Design</h2>
<p>Design is more than making things look good. It is a way of communicating ideas, questioning existing conditions, and exploring how we might make a difference.</p>
<p><strong>Our starting question:</strong> What can design do for people and communities?</p>
<p><span class="sd-number">01</span> / Observe   <span class="sd-number">02</span> / Question   <span class="sd-number">03</span> / Imagine</p>
</header>
<section class="sd-section">
<div class="sd-label">Start Here</div>
<h3>What Is Social Design?</h3>
<p>Social design is an approach to design that places people, communities, and social needs at the center of the creative process. Rather than focusing exclusively on selling a product or promoting a brand, social design considers how visual communication, objects, services, and environments might contribute to positive social change.</p>
<p>Social designers might address issues such as access to education, environmental sustainability, public health, accessibility, inequality, or the way communities understand and respond to a shared problem.</p>
<div class="sd-callout">
<strong>The big idea</strong>
<p>Social design begins by asking not only <em>"What should I make?"</em> but also <em>"Who is this for, what need does it address, and what might change as a result?"</em></p>
</div>
<p>Social design can take many forms. A poster might make an overlooked issue visible. An infographic might help people understand complicated information. A public installation might invite conversation. A campaign might connect people with resources or encourage collective action.</p>
<p>In this course, we will explore graphic design as a tool for communicating ideas, examining social issues, and imagining alternatives to the way things currently work.</p>
</section>

<section class="sd-section">
<div class="sd-label">Core Concepts</div>
<h3>Design With a Purpose</h3>
<p>All design communicates something, whether intentionally or not. Social design asks us to think carefully about what we communicate, whom we reach, and what effects our work may have.</p>
<div class="sd-grid">
<div class="sd-card">
<h4>People Before Aesthetics</h4>
<p>A visually impressive design is not necessarily an effective one. We must consider whether people can understand, access, and use the information we provide.</p>
</div>
<div class="sd-card">
<h4>Context Matters</h4>
<p>Design does not exist in a vacuum. History, culture, economics, politics, and lived experiences can all influence how a message is interpreted.</p>
</div>
<div class="sd-card">
<h4>Communication Can Influence</h4>
<p>Typography, imagery, color, scale, and composition influence what people notice and how they interpret a message.</p>
</div>
<div class="sd-card">
<h4>Intent Is Not Impact</h4>
<p>A designer may have good intentions, but the outcome can still be confusing, exclusionary, or ineffective. Research, feedback, and revision matter.</p>
</div>
</div>
</section>
<section class="sd-section">
<div class="sd-label">Visual Communication</div>
<h3>Where Do We See Social Design?</h3>
<p>Look around your community and consider how design influences everyday life.</p>
<div class="sd-grid">
<div class="sd-card">
<h4>Public Awareness</h4>
<p>Posters and campaigns that communicate information about health, safety, civic participation, or community needs.</p>
</div>
<div class="sd-card">
<h4>Environmental Action</h4>
<p>Infographics and visual campaigns that help audiences understand waste, energy use, climate, or resource consumption.</p>
</div>
<div class="sd-card">
<h4>Community &amp; Culture</h4>
<p>Exhibitions, publications, and visual identities that preserve local histories, share cultural experiences, or make overlooked perspectives visible.</p>
</div>
<div class="sd-card">
<h4>Access &amp; Inclusion</h4>
<p>Wayfinding, accessible information, and inclusive communication that help more people participate in public life.</p>
</div>
</div>
</section>
<section class="sd-section">
<div class="sd-label">Think Like a Designer</div>
<h3>Five Questions to Ask About Any Project</h3>
<div class="sd-step">
<div class="sd-stepnum">1</div>
<div><h4>What is the issue?</h4><p>What situation, need, or opportunity is the project responding to?</p></div>
</div>
<div class="sd-step">
<div class="sd-stepnum">2</div>
<div><h4>Who is affected?</h4><p>Who experiences the issue directly? Whose perspective might be missing?</p></div>
</div>
<div class="sd-step">
<div class="sd-stepnum">3</div>
<div><h4>What should the audience understand?</h4><p>What is the central message, and what information does the audience need?</p></div>
</div>
<div class="sd-step">
<div class="sd-stepnum">4</div>
<div><h4>What could design contribute?</h4><p>Could visual communication inform, invite participation, challenge assumptions, or connect people with resources?</p></div>
</div>
<div class="sd-step">
<div class="sd-stepnum">5</div>
<div><h4>How would we know it worked?</h4><p>What evidence would suggest the design reached its audience or helped achieve its intended goal?</p></div>
</div>
</section>
<section class="sd-section">
<div class="sd-label">In-Class Activity</div>
<h3>Design Detective: Find a Problem Worth Addressing</h3>
<p><strong>Goal:</strong> Practice observing everyday situations through the lens of social design. This is an exploratory exercise, not a demand to solve a complex social problem in one class session.</p>
<div class="sd-teal">
<strong>Your challenge</strong>
<p>Identify an issue that affects a community, group, or shared environment. Then propose one way graphic design or visual communication might help address it.</p>
</div>
<div class="sd-step">
<div class="sd-stepnum">1</div>
<div><h4>Observe</h4><p>Individually, brainstorm three issues you have noticed at school, in your community, online, or in everyday life. Choose one to explore.</p></div>
</div>
<div class="sd-step">
<div class="sd-stepnum">2</div>
<div><h4>Define the audience</h4><p>Identify who is affected and who would need to see, understand, or use the proposed design.</p></div>
</div>
<div class="sd-step">
<div class="sd-stepnum">3</div>
<div><h4>Choose a format</h4><p>Suggest a poster, infographic, social media campaign, publication, exhibition, or another form of visual communication.</p></div>
</div>
<div class="sd-step">
<div class="sd-stepnum">4</div>
<div><h4>Sketch the idea</h4><p>Create a quick thumbnail sketch. Include a possible headline, a visual idea, and a call to action or takeaway.</p></div>
</div>
<div class="sd-step">
<div class="sd-stepnum">5</div>
<div><h4>Share and discuss</h4><p>Present your idea to a partner or the class. Explain the problem, the audience, and how your proposed design could help.</p></div>
</div>
<div class="sd-callout">
<strong>Discussion prompt</strong>
<p>Is raising awareness enough to create change, or does an effective design need to do something more? What might that "something more" look like?</p>
</div>
</section>
<section class="sd-section">
<div class="sd-label">Critique &amp; Reflection</div>
<h3>How We Discuss Social Issues</h3>
<p>Social Design Studio may involve topics that are personal, controversial, or emotionally challenging. Our goal is to create thoughtful design and thoughtful discussion, even when we disagree.</p>
<ul class="sd-list">
<li>Critique the design and the idea, not the worth of the person who created it.</li>
<li>Explain your observations and support your opinions with reasons.</li>
<li>Listen to perspectives that differ from your own.</li>
<li>Consider who is represented, who is missing, and how an audience might interpret the work.</li>
<li>Remain respectful when discussing sensitive issues. Hate speech and targeted harassment are not acceptable.</li>
<li>Ask questions before making assumptions about another person's experience.</li>
</ul>
<p>As outlined in our course materials, please speak with your instructor if a topic or activity causes concern. Constructive dialogue is encouraged, but respectful participation is essential.</p>
</section>
