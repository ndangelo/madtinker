<h1>From Brand Awareness to Campaign Action</h1>
<p>You’ve already developed a brand and explored social platforms. Now you’ll bring those pieces together in a focused campaign: one audience, one clear objective, a consistent message, and content adapted for Facebook, Instagram, X, and TikTok.</p>
<div class="amde-meta">
<div><strong>Your focus</strong>Promote a specific offer, event, product, service, or action.</div>
<div><strong>Your platforms</strong>Facebook · Instagram · X · TikTok</div>
<div><strong>Your finish line</strong>A planned two-week campaign scheduled with Adobe Express.</div>
</div>
</header>
<p>A campaign is more than a group of attractive posts. It is a coordinated sequence of messages designed to help a particular audience understand something, care about it, and take a next step.</p>
<div class="amde-callout">
<strong>Keep your existing brand.</strong> This project builds on the brand identity and platform work you completed earlier in the semester. You are not starting over or creating an entirely new brand. You are developing a specific campaign for that brand.
    </div>
<h2>The campaign, step by step</h2>
<div class="amde-grid">
<article class="amde-card"><div class="amde-num">Step 01</div><h3>Choose the campaign</h3><p>Decide exactly what you want to promote and what you want your audience to do. Make the focus specific enough to explain in one sentence.</p></article>
<article class="amde-card"><div class="amde-num">Step 02</div><h3>Define the audience and message</h3><p>Identify the people most likely to care, what matters to them, what might stop them from acting, and the message that connects their needs to your offer.</p></article>
<article class="amde-card"><div class="amde-num">Step 03</div><h3>Adapt for four platforms</h3><p>Keep the campaign idea consistent while adapting the format, pacing, voice, and presentation for Facebook, Instagram, X, and TikTok.</p></article>
<article class="amde-card"><div class="amde-num">Step 04</div><h3>Plan the content sequence</h3><p>Give each post a job. Introduce the campaign, demonstrate value, answer questions, provide proof, or remind people how to take action.</p></article>
<article class="amde-card"><div class="amde-num">Step 05</div><h3>Produce and schedule</h3><p>Prepare your graphics, videos, captions, links, and other assets. Review each post carefully, then schedule the campaign in Adobe Express.</p></article>
<article class="amde-card"><div class="amde-num">Step 06</div><h3>Present and evaluate</h3><p>Explain why you made your choices, how the platforms work together, and which measurements would help you judge the campaign’s success.</p></article>

<h2>01 / What makes a campaign?</h2>
<p>Your brand is the ongoing identity. A campaign is a focused push with a specific purpose and a defined period of activity.</p>
<p>A broad goal such as “get more followers” does not tell you what to create. A campaign objective should help you make decisions about the audience, message, posts, and call to action.</p>
<div class="amde-example">
<div class="amde-label">Worked example</div>
<p><strong>Too broad:</strong> “I want more people to know about my ceramics business.”</p>
<p><strong>More focused:</strong> “Over two weeks, encourage local adults who enjoy hands-on creative activities to register for a beginner pottery workshop.”</p>
<p><strong>Primary call to action:</strong> “See the workshop details and reserve your place.”</p>
</div>
<h3>Try this objective formula</h3>
<p>“This campaign will help <em>[audience]</em> understand or value <em>[offer]</em> so they will <em>[action]</em> during <em>[period]</em>.”</p>
<p>Choose one primary action: register, visit, purchase, inquire, download, attend, sign up, or another action that makes sense for your brand. Your supporting content can vary, but the campaign should not ask the audience to do five unrelated things.</p>
<h2>02 / Audience, insight, and message</h2>
<p>“Everyone” is not a useful target audience. Think about the people most likely to care about this specific campaign and the reason they might respond.</p>
<ol class="amde-steps">
<li><strong>Describe your audience.</strong> Include relevant context such as interests, needs, habits, location, or familiarity with your brand.</li>
<li><strong>Identify a motivation.</strong> What problem, desire, question, or opportunity makes your offer relevant?</li>
<li><strong>Anticipate barriers.</strong> What might stop someone from noticing, trusting, understanding, or acting on your message?</li>
<li><strong>Write an insight.</strong> Complete the thought: “Our audience is likely to respond because…” Explain the connection between their motivation and your offer.</li>
<li><strong>Choose a message and proof.</strong> State the benefit clearly, then support it with a demonstration, useful detail, example, or credible evidence.</li>
</ol>
<div class="amde-example">
<div class="amde-label">Worked example</div>
<p><strong>Audience:</strong> Local adults who enjoy making things but have never tried pottery.</p>
<p><strong>Possible barrier:</strong> “I’m not artistic, so I’ll be bad at it.”</p>
<p><strong>Message:</strong> “Try something new—no pottery experience needed.”</p>
<p><strong>Proof:</strong> A short demonstration of the first step, plus examples of beginner work.</p>
<p><strong>CTA:</strong> “Explore the workshop and reserve your place.”</p>
</div>
<h2>03 / One campaign, four platforms</h2>
<p>Cross-platform content is not simply copy-and-paste. Keep the core campaign idea, visual identity, offer, and main call to action consistent—but make each execution feel appropriate to its platform.</p>
<div class="amde-grid">
<article class="amde-platform"><h3>Facebook</h3><p>Useful for event details, community context, practical information, and sharing. Make important details easy to find: what, where, when, and how to respond.</p><p class="amde-small"><strong>Example:</strong> An event-oriented post with a clear workshop description, date, location, and registration link.</p></article>
<article class="amde-platform"><h3>Instagram</h3><p>Use visual storytelling, carousels, Reels, and a recognizable visual style. Each image or frame should contribute something, rather than repeating the same message.</p><p class="amde-small"><strong>Example:</strong> A carousel showing the workshop process, what beginners will make, and a final slide with the date and registration CTA.</p></article>
<article class="amde-platform"><h3>X</h3><p>Make the point quickly. Use concise copy, timely updates, relevant conversation, and a clear destination when you need people to learn more.</p><p class="amde-small"><strong>Example:</strong> “Never tried pottery? Our beginner workshop walks you through your first cup, step by step. Details: [link].”</p></article>
<article class="amde-platform"><h3>TikTok</h3><p>Consider a demonstration, short story, transformation, or behind-the-scenes moment. Give viewers a reason to keep watching, then make the next step clear.</p><p class="amde-small"><strong>Example:</strong> A vertical video showing clay, a brief instructor demonstration, a finished beginner cup, and an on-screen workshop CTA.</p></article>
</div>
<div class="amde-callout">
<strong>Keep the idea consistent; adapt the execution.</strong> If the same graphic and caption appear unchanged on all four platforms, ask what each version contributes that the others do not.
    </div>

<h3>Organize your campaign assets</h3>
<p>Before scheduling, gather the materials you will actually use. A simple folder structure can help:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>01 — Brand:</strong> logo, colors, fonts, style guide.</li>
<li><strong>02 — Images:</strong> approved photos and graphics.</li>
<li><strong>03 — Video:</strong> clips, edits, captions, subtitles.</li>
<li><strong>04 — Copy and links:</strong> captions, hashtags where useful, destination URLs, key details.</li>
<li><strong>05 — Final exports:</strong> platform-ready versions of each asset.</li>
</ul>
<p>Use original or properly licensed images, video, and music. Check permissions before featuring identifiable people when appropriate. Make content accessible: use readable text, sufficient contrast, useful alt text where supported, and captions or subtitles for video.</p>
<h2>05 / Produce and schedule in Adobe Express</h2>
<p>Scheduling is a production step—not a replacement for strategy. Each post should have the correct creative, caption, platform, publication date, destination link, and call to action.</p>
<ol class="amde-steps">
<li><strong>Finish the brief first.</strong> Use the audience, objective, and message to guide creative decisions.</li>
<li><strong>Prepare each platform version.</strong> Check composition, focal point, legibility on a phone, and any text that may be covered by interface elements.</li>
<li><strong>Write the caption.</strong> Let the visual attract attention; use the caption to add context and clarify the next step.</li>
<li><strong>Proofread the details.</strong> Check dates, prices, names, locations, claims, spelling, and URLs. Test links and make sure the destination page matches the post.</li>
<li><strong>Check accessibility.</strong> Add captions/subtitles to video and ensure the post still makes sense without sound.</li>
<li><strong>Schedule carefully.</strong> Confirm the connected account, platform, date/time, caption, media, and preview before scheduling.</li>
<li><strong>Review the whole calendar.</strong> Look for accidental repetition, gaps, inconsistent messages, and a CTA that disappears from the campaign.</li>
</ol>
<div class="amde-example">
<div class="amde-label">A simple caption framework</div>
<p><strong>Hook + Value + Proof or Detail + CTA</strong></p>
<p>“Your first pottery class doesn’t need to be perfect. Learn the basics of shaping clay in a guided beginner workshop. [Add only accurate details about materials, experience, date, and location.] See the details and reserve your place: [link].”</p>
</div>

<h2>06 / Evaluate and present your campaign</h2>
<p>Your presentation should explain the thinking behind the work, not just show a gallery of finished posts. Connect your objective to the content and to the way you would evaluate the results.</p>
<h3>Match the measurement to the goal</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Registrations or attendance:</strong> track sign-ups or attendance for an event.</li>
<li><strong>Sales:</strong> track purchases or qualified inquiries where relevant.</li>
<li><strong>Website traffic:</strong> track link clicks and visits to the intended page.</li>
<li><strong>Engagement or awareness:</strong> consider reach, views, saves, shares, meaningful replies, or video completion—depending on the campaign and the data available.</li>
</ul>
<div class="amde-callout">
<strong>Output is not the same as outcome.</strong> Publishing ten posts is an output. Generating registrations is an outcome. Choose measurements that tell you whether the campaign is achieving its objective.
    </div>
<p>If your campaign has not run yet, do not invent results. Explain what you would measure, what would count as a useful signal, and what you might revise if the campaign did not perform as expected.</p>
<p>For example, if a video receives many views but few people register, investigate the offer details, CTA, link, and registration page before deciding that the answer is simply to post more often.</p>

<h2>Before you submit: campaign self-check</h2>
<ul class="amde-checklist">
<li>I can explain the campaign in one clear sentence.</li>
<li>The campaign promotes a specific offer, event, product, service, or action.</li>
<li>The target audience is focused and connected to the objective.</li>
<li>The central message is clear, credible, and supported by relevant details.</li>
<li>Facebook, Instagram, X, and TikTok are included, with platform-specific adaptations.</li>
<li>Each post has a purpose in the overall sequence.</li>
<li>Assets, captions, dates, details, and destination links are ready and checked.</li>
<li>The two-week schedule is coherent and realistic.</li>
<li>The campaign uses accessible content and appropriate image/video permissions.</li>
<li>The evaluation plan uses measures that fit the campaign objective.</li>
</ul>
